## Signature Work

### Credit Risk & Causal Inference — Fleetcor ($70M+ Exposure)
`Causal Inference` `Propensity Score Matching` `Ridge Regression` `Random Forest` `Tableau`

High-risk commercial accounts were accumulating credit exposure visible only at write-off. The intervention *looked* effective — but selection bias made the raw comparison meaningless.

**What I did:** Applied Propensity Score Matching to control for selection effects, Ridge Regression to isolate the Average Treatment Effect per account, and a Random Forest classifier to score which treated customers would still churn — producing an auditable, statistically defensible case for scaling the program.

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Write-off savings identified | ~$70M |
| Avg. treatment effect per account | -$785 |
| Churn prediction accuracy | 89% |

> This is the kind of analysis that survives a CFO's follow-up questions. It doesn't just say "the program works" — it says *by how much, for whom, and why.*

> ### Public Transit Operations — CT Transit New Haven Corridor
> `Operations Research` `Queuing Theory (Little's Law)` `Demand Forecasting` `Centre of Gravity`
>
> Fleet deployment was reactive. No quantitative basis for frequency decisions. No model connecting hub location to deadhead cost.
>
> **What I did:** Modeled optimal fleet frequency using Little's Law, built demand forecasts with confidence intervals for budget stress-testing, applied Centre-of-Gravity analysis to reposition maintenance hubs — eliminating the deadhead problem at its root rather than patching dispatch.
>
> | Outcome | Result |
> |---|---|
> | Passenger wait time reduction | 25–28% |
> | Daily ridership gain | 320 → 500 passengers/day |
> | Annualized route revenue impact | $100K+ |

> ### Retail Supply Chain — Supplier Quality & Cost Analytics
> `Supply Chain Analytics` `Pareto Analysis` `SPC` `Total Cost Modeling`
>
> In a multi-country supply chain modeled on IKEA's structure, quality failures were tracked in aggregate — masking that 20% of suppliers drove 75–80% of all defects.
>
> **What I did:** Pareto segmentation to surface the concentration, nonconformance rate trending to separate systematic from isolated failures, and a total landed cost model to quantify the P&L value of each improvement scenario — so procurement decisions had a number attached, not just a narrative.
>
> | Outcome | Result |
> |---|---|
> | Defect concentration surfaced | Top 20% of suppliers → 75–80% of failures |
> | Missing parts rate reduction target | 25–30% |
> | Total supply chain cost reduction pathway | 8–12% |
